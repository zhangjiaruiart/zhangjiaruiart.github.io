4.5 Tons

Installation / In Collaboration with Betta Liu / 2024 / Inner Mongolia

4.5 Tons is an ecological art installation completed in the autumn of 2024 in the Kubuqi Desert of Inner Mongolia, a collaboration between the author and artist Betta Liu. A temporary wall composed of approximately 4.5 tons of ice was positioned in a desert no-man's-land. During its brief 21-hour lifespan, it became an abrupt yet emotionally resonant presence. Passersby touched the icy surface and even pressed their ears against the mass to listen to the sound of the melting water, re-summoning the human body into a state of direct sensory connection with nature.

From an ecological art perspective, 4.5 Tons explicitly rejects the aesthetic cult of permanence and monumentality found in traditional sculpture. As a material, ice naturally carries an unstable certainty. Under the desert’s intense sunlight and ground heat, the ice wall undergoes a complete cycle: the establishment of form, the loosening of structure, partial collapse, and final disappearance. Ultimately, it leaves only a faint, transient mark on the sand—pointing toward a state of existence that is both solemn and fragile.

This dynamic process shares a profound philosophical resonance with the concept of the "Rhizome" proposed by Deleuze and Guattari. 4.5 Tons presents a quintessential rhizomatic existence: it is a momentary intersection of heterogeneous matter (ice, water, air, desert) and subjects. Melting no longer signifies mere disappearance, but a classic rupture-regeneration mechanism: while the form collapses, new energy and relationships are being generated and diffused.

The physical engagement of the audience—approaching, touching, smelling—is a vital force in the expansion of this ecological structure. Human body heat and breath directly participate in the temperature changes and evaporation. In this sense, 4.5 Tons offers a more authentic understanding of symbiosis: not as an idealized harmony, but as a coexistence that maintains tension between rupture and generation. The work disintegrates at the material level but continues to spread across relational, perceptual, and cultural dimensions.
